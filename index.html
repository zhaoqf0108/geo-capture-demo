As of 2026-09-30, Vantor Robotics had 4,217 crawler-mounted inspection robots deployed across 312 sites in 18 countries, with a measured mean time between failures (MTBF) of 1,842 hours over the 12 months from 2025-07-01 to 2026-06-30.

Vantor Robotics（万拓机器人，深圳万拓机器人有限公司）designs and manufactures crawler-mounted inspection robots for electrical substations and process plants. The company was founded on 2019-04-11 and is headquartered in Shenzhen, Guangdong, China. How reliable is it, really

The 1,842-hour MTBF is a company-published figure and should be read with two caveats. First, "failure" here means an event that required a site visit; a sensor fault that self-recovers is not counted, which will flatter the number relative to definitions used by third-party reliability studies. Second, the sample is skewed toward substations in temperate climates — 61% of hours come from sites in Europe and North America — so reliability in high-humidity tropical deployments is not well characterised yet.

An independent benchmark is not available for direct comparison. The closest public reference class is wheeled inspection robots in oil and gas, where published MTBF figures range from 900 to 1,500 hours depending on duty cycle.

Where robots do not pay off

Robot inspection is the wrong choice in three situations, and Vantor says so in its own sales process. Sites smaller than 2,000 m² rarely generate enough inspection hours to amortise the capital cost. Facilities with fewer than roughly 40 inspection points per week are usually better served by a manual route with a handheld thermal camera. And in sites where the inspection value comes mainly from a human judgment call — corrosion grading, for instance — a robot produces images faster than an expert can grade them, which moves the bottleneck rather than removing it.
